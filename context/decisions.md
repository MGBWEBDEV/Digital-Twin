| D24 | APPROVED | Fidelity axis. Both models share the same water balance, simulation time step, controller interface and per-pump calibrated flow rates. **Model 1** treats the rig as ideal: pumps start and stop instantly, the sensor switches exactly at its level, communication is instantaneous. **Model 2** = Model 1 plus the real-world effects that are measurable on the rig. Candidates: tube priming (water needed to fill the tubing before any reaches the syringe), run-on after OFF, the actual sensor trigger point and sensor reporting delay (including different switch-on/switch-off levels when draining), and communication/reaction delay (expected negligible). Inclusion rule: an effect enters Model 2 only if WP04 shows it larger than the syringe reading resolution (volumes) or the log timestamp resolution (times); otherwise it is documented as excluded. Not modelled unless data require it: head-dependent flow, random run-to-run noise (variability is measured and used for tolerances). Measurement rules: rig volumes are read visually after the water has settled; timing comes from host logs; on the rig, "overflow" means a settled level above a marked safe limit below capacity, and real overflow is only computed in the models, never caused physically; where a peak cannot be read as a settled level, the rig check is undetermined | Supersedes D08; narrows D13. The time-step size and equation form are deliberately not the fidelity difference (they can be mathematically equivalent). If no effect qualifies, the finding "Model 1 is sufficient at this rig's resolution" is recorded and a fallback is decided then; no effect is invented. Affects WP04, WP05, WP08, WP11. | D27 | APPROVED | Ground truth and repetitions. (1) Safety net outside the controller: the ESP32 firmware switches any pump OFF after a maximum ON time, independent of the host (reuses StepExample's self-expiring timed step). The maximum time is chosen so that starting level + intake rate × maximum time stays below the syringe's physical capacity, using WP04 calibration; overflow is then physically impossible for any controller. (2) Both simulation adapters implement the identical cut-off, so rig and models are compared under the same protection. (3) A safety-net cut-off is logged and scored as an FR08 failure of the controller, never as a pass. (4) With the net in place, faulty and reference controllers are run on the rig to obtain rig verdicts (ground truth for D25); results the rig cannot establish (e.g. an unreadable transient peak) are undetermined. (5) Repetitions: in WP04 each basic characterization measurement is repeated 5 times; if the settled readings agree within the syringe reading resolution, the rig is treated as consistent. Controller experiments then run each controller × scenario once on the rig; only results whose margin to the safe mark is within the spread seen in WP04 are repeated, up to 5 times, and remain undetermined if they still disagree. Simulations are deterministic and run once | Makes rig verdicts available safely; keeps lab effort to roughly 15–20 experiment runs plus near-limit repeats. Assumption to state as a limitation: consistency over 5 repetitions is taken to persist. Physical runs still require researcher approval per run plan. Affects WP04, WP08, WP09, WP10, WP11, WP13, WP15 | 2026-11-25, researcher (via Claude Code session) |
